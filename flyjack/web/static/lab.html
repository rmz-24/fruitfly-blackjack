    Tournament

    The fly and four reference strategies each play the same number of hands, dealt from the same random seed. Uses the readout (each decision sees a fresh held-out neural trial).

    How the fly learned

    Expected winnings per hand of the fly's current strategy while it plays and dopamine updates its Kenyon cell → output neuron synapses. Five independently trained flies.

    Train a new fly

    Start from a naive mushroom body and watch it learn. Takes seconds. The new fly can then play in every tab (switch “Fly” in the header). Training never overwrites the saved results.

    How the fly plays Blackjack

    1. Cards as odors. The state (your total, dealer's upcard, soft/hard) becomes a combination of glomerulus channels. Projection neurons in active channels fire at 150 Hz for 200 ms. Nearby totals share channels, so they “smell” alike.
    2. The real wiring. The whole FlyWire connectome is simulated as spiking neurons. Projection neurons drive the Kenyon cells of the mushroom body; about 7% of them respond to any one state.
    3. Dopamine learning. Two output pools, HIT and STAND, read the Kenyon cells. After each choice a dopamine-like prediction error strengthens or weakens the synapses of the chosen pool from the active Kenyon cells.
    4. The closed loop. The learned weights were installed as real synapses onto two pairs of mushroom-body output neurons (MBON01 = HIT, MBON03 = STAND). In “Spiking brain” mode each decision runs the whole brain for 200 ms, and the fly hits if its HIT neurons fire more spikes.
